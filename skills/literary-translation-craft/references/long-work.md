# Plan for long literary work

This is an editorial workflow, not an executable pipeline or authorization to use other providers or agents. Apply it only to the scope requested and material actually available. Use the user's chosen models and permitted execution environment; work in a single session if delegation or other models are unavailable or not authorized.

## 1. Prepare a revisable source map

An editor with suitable language and literary capability coordinates the work. Identify the edition and scope; map chapters or scenes, narrators, changes of viewpoint, character knowledge, voices, relationships, forms of address, and plot-bearing repetitions. Mark dialogue, poetry, wordplay, cultural references, significant ambiguity, and structural or formatting constraints as potential difficulties. Do not claim to have read or mapped material not actually inspected.

Keep the [brief](brief.md) and glossary versioned. Each important name, alias, term, image, or stylistic decision records its sense, choice, supporting source location, status, and unresolved alternatives. The original is the authority: summaries and glossary entries may be mistaken and must be corrected when the source contradicts them. Track occurrences by meaning rather than imposing global string replacement.

For a substantial run, use the optional [execution record](execution-record.json). Mark each stage `done`, `partial`, `not_run`, or `not_applicable` and link actual outputs or decisions; a planned stage is not completed work. Record exact skill/reference revisions read and the available context. Role entries identify the actual model, settings when known, and whether it saw other translations. A new role name does not make the same model independent. Short excerpts can state these facts in prose.

## 2. Pilot, then route by observed difficulty

Before scaling a long work, translate representative passages that expose contrasting voices and forms, such as narration, dialogue, and a difficult scene. Compare each against the original and read it aloud or as target-language prose; record actual weaknesses, revise the brief or approach, and repeat a targeted passage if needed. The pilot is a calibration, not proof of whole-book quality.

If multiple authorized models are available, use a capable coordinator/editor to plan, evaluate and resolve difficult cases. A less costly model may draft passages only when a representative pilot indicates adequate performance and the measured route remains worthwhile after planning, audits and actual corrections. Do not substitute on draft price alone. Route wordplay, verse, structural ambiguity, plot-critical scenes, or inadequate drafts to a more capable translator or editor. Do not guess capability from price or a model name. If a stronger model is unavailable, translate with the available model and disclose substantive unresolved difficulty. Record the model and role actually used; do not imply a specific model or independent reviewer when absent.

## 3. Translate in context

Split at scene and paragraph boundaries with enough source context for voice, pronouns, chronology, and imagery. Chunks should be small enough for complete output, not so small that context is lost. Supply the exact source passage, clearly marked read-only neighboring source, relevant passport/glossary entries, and, when needed, the accepted ending of the preceding passage. Neighboring context is not extra text to translate. See [context handoff](context-handoff.md) for the record shape and a worked example.

Keep unresolved terms or ambiguous readings outside the literary text as proposals. A coordinator reconciles them against the original and updates the versioned decisions. Preserve actual completed chunks and their input/decision versions for resumption.

Each passage keeps its source ID and output ID through translation, review and assembly. Before work begins, check that the selected source and any supplied historical translation cover the same events and boundaries; equal paragraph numbers or matching lengths are insufficient. Check output completeness before marking a chunk done. A clipped output, invented connective, or translated neighbor requires repair before assembly.

## 4. Review and assemble

First compare the translation with the original for omissions, additions, actors, events, negation, modality, chronology, references, and plot-bearing repetition. Then read connected target-language passages for voice, rhythm, dialogue, scene transitions, and natural syntax. Investigate candidate issues individually: a glossary match or fluent prose cannot prove fidelity. A second call to the same model is self-review; name independent review or human proofreading only if it actually occurred.

Adjudicate substantive findings using source context. For a local defect, request and apply the accepted span replacement rather than regenerating every reviewed passage; reread the complete altered passage and its boundaries. A review with no actionable findings does not require an editor generation and does not close earlier supported or unresolved issues. Assemble the accepted passages in reading order; check boundaries, chapter continuity, names, forms of address, and recurring images across the whole requested scope. When a glossary or passport decision changes, identify affected occurrences by meaning and inspect those passages before editing or retranslation. Keep a significant unresolved issue open.

Stop when the requested scope is closed. Additional full passes need a concrete remaining risk. Report actual source and target coverage, models and roles used, verification stage, unresolved issues, and whether the final result received qualified human review. A preference test and technical checks cannot establish universal literary quality.

When critique and fluency editing disagree, identify which action, viewpoint, uncertainty or literary effect each alternative preserves. Keep the supported version rather than merging every suggestion. Re-run only the affected passage and its boundary context; stop cycling when changes are preferences without a demonstrated gain.
