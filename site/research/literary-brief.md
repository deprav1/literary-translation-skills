# Editorial brief for the pilot excerpts

Prepared for the v4 review on 2026-10-29 after reading the three source stories linked in [literary-review.json](literary-review.json). This is a model-authored, revisable editorial brief, not an authoritative interpretation or human review.

## Poe: The Tell-Tale Heart

- A first-person narrator addresses an implied listener, urgently defending sanity while describing murder and a growing obsession. Do not turn the narrator's conviction into an objective diagnosis.
- Preserve the unstable movement between deliberation, triumph, fear, and the final outburst. Short repeated clauses and emphatic apostrophes carry the rhythm.
- Recurring images: the old man's vulture-like eye and its film, the dark lantern and narrow ray, the apparent heartbeat and the sound of a watch. `Death watches in the wall` refers to the ticking of deathwatch beetles; it is not a literal clock.
- Do not insert an explanation of the narrator's gender, the source of the heartbeat, or the old man's thoughts beyond what the text supports.

## Wilde: The Doer of Good

- The narrator's deliberately repetitive, biblical cadence frames three encounters. Preserve parallel openings, concrete details and the exchanges; leave the parable's judgment to the reader.
- The unnamed central figure is addressed as «Ты» in the working Russian rendering. The young people and woman are distinct speakers. Maintain the distinction between his questions and their replies.
- Terms are contextual: leper = person once afflicted with leprosy; blind = person whose sight was restored; `the way` is the figurative path the woman describes. Avoid adding an explanatory sermon.
- A local choice to use capitalized pronouns for the central figure is editorial and should not be treated as a fact of the English original's theology.

## Poe: The Black Cat

- The first-person narrator addresses the reader on the eve of death; his explanatory confidence and his violence coexist with uncertainty about events. Preserve the shifts in self-justification and do not resolve supernatural ambiguity.
- Pluto is the first cat; a second similar cat appears later. The narrator alone feeds Pluto. Keep animal, spouse, and narrator distinct; his early claim of tenderness contrasts with later cruelty.
- Preserve the narration’s rising repetitions and rhetorical questions. Short website excerpts are selected from distinct parts of the story, not a replacement for its full context.

## Checks and boundaries

Compare every reviewed excerpt with its exact source and surrounding story, then read the Russian for voice and continuity. Preserve meaningful repetitions; an overlap in the site corpus is not permission to merge excerpts. Decisions and actual coverage are recorded separately. These three short stories provide context for the pilot, not evidence about other works.
