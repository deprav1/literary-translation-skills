# Conditional Russian editing checks

Use these questions for Russian prose, not as automatic rewrite rules. They are original editorial checks; the project does not claim that a named translator prescribed this list.

- **Pronouns:** Is the possessive redundant, or does it distinguish owners, mark contrast, or sustain a voice? Delete only after checking referents and emphasis.
- **Verbs and nominalizations:** Would a verb improve an accidental bureaucratic construction? Keep nominal or passive forms when institutional speech, distance, or an unknown agent is intentional.
- **Word order:** Locate topic, focus, contrast, and sentence stress. Russian permits several arrangements; new information does not invariably belong at the end.
- **Idioms:** Match meaning, emotional force, speaker, period, and cultural setting. Avoid importing an unexplained Russian institution or folklore; familiar idioms are not all forbidden merely because the scene is abroad.
- **Rhythm:** Read clause boundaries, repetitions, roughness, and pauses against the source. A shorter, smoother sentence is not necessarily better.
- **Quotation and poetry:** Verify a claimed published rendering against an identified edition. Do not substitute a remembered quotation or add explanatory notes without a reason within the task. Set priorities for verse rather than imposing rhyme or meter on every poem.

Example: *He put his hand over hers, not over the letter.* needs the distinction between hands and the letter. Removing every possessive can erase the scene. *It was decided that no names would be recorded* may deliberately conceal the deciding agent; an active rewrite must not invent one.
